## Calculation and source boundaries

1 day uses the latest two completed daily closes; 3 days requires four bars;
1 week requires six bars for five trading-session returns. All included rows
use the same expected dates from a NYSE holiday calendar for 2025–2028.
New York midnight, not UTC midnight or the market close, controls availability.
Every required intermediate bar must exist. Missing/invalid bars are not
shortened into another period, forward-filled or treated as zero.
Returns are (last close / first close - 1) × 100, split-adjusted, excluding
separate dividends and FX. Daily session labels never become intraday samples.
The holiday calendar includes scheduled closures and the 9 January 2025
exception. Future extraordinary closures can require a calendar update;
an empty expected day stops loading instead of silently skipping it.

The first two/four/six required files progressively enable 1D/3D/1W. The
current New York date is a generation identifier. Endpoints from different
generations are not mixed for the heatmap. A prior complete map is retained
and explicitly dated during a partial update. Three recent generations are
retained. The loader shares the existing Massive allowance: four local
requests per minute, with at least 16 seconds between its own requests and
provider/backoff handling. Another tool can consume that same allowance.
No additional paid service or broker connection is used.

The daily chart cache also receives the validated OHLC rows in the same
transaction as its saved progress checkpoint, keeping previously loaded
longer histories. The stored response is reduced to the supported shortlist;
raw full-market payloads are not archived. Responses are bounded at 8 MB;
unadjusted data, duplicate symbols, wrong-session timestamps and invalid
OHLC/volume are rejected or withheld. Cache corruption never produces prices.
