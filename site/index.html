    Dates, sources and how this works

    One day means one completed US trading session, three days means three, and a week means five. Returns compare split-adjusted closing prices using matching dates. Weekends and scheduled exchange holidays are skipped. No dividends or currency conversion are included.

    Massive Basic supplies past days after midnight New York time, so these are end-of-day results, not live or rolling 24-hour returns. The loader makes one shared market-wide request per date, paced at least 16 seconds apart and subject to the existing shared four-request local budget. Pausing or closing preserves progress. Reopening resumes unless you paused. No data is collected while your Mac is off.

    Each company needs all daily bars for the selected period. Missing, suspended or unsupported listings stay gray. Calendar checked for 2025–2028; exceptional closures may require an update. This directory is a shortlist, not the whole market. Browsing groups are editorial.

    How to find a suitable historical file
    1. Start with a historical-price export from a provider or finance site you already use and are allowed to download from. Check its actual date range: a company listed recently will have less history.

    What these accounts cover

    Selected consolidated US-GAAP figures in US dollars from SEC Company Facts. This first version supports eligible US 10-K and 10-Q filers. Custom company tags and IFRS figures stay unavailable. These are selected facts, not complete financial statements.

    HOW IT WORKS / WHAT IT MEANS

    How to read provider ratios

    P/E ratio compares the share price with earnings per share. It may not help when earnings are negative or missing.

    Net margin shows how much revenue is left as profit. Compare companies that do similar things.

    Debt / equity depends on accounting rules and the industry. It does not explain all of a company’s risk.

    52-week range shows past prices. It does not tell you what the stock should be worth.

    UNDERSTANDING THE ECONOMY / A LINK DOES NOT PROVE A CAUSE

    What the economy could mean for a company

    01 / DEMANDEconomic growth & spending

    A growing economy may increase demand. A company’s sales also depend on its prices, competitors and customers abroad.

    02 / COSTSInflation & profit margins

    Rising costs can reduce profits. The effect depends on contracts and whether a company can raise its prices.

    03 / LABOURUnemployment & wages

    Jobs and wages affect customer spending and company costs. Published employment figures are estimates and may change.

    04 / FINANCINGInterest rates & company value

    Borrowing costs affect companies and their value. The World Bank lending rate is not the rate set by the Bank of England or US Federal Reserve.

    Before using an economic figure, check its date, country, meaning, source and comparison period. The UK and US may measure things differently.

    02 / PAST PRICES & CHART INDICATORS

    Moving averages smooth prices over 20 or 50 trading days. RSI measures recent gains and losses over 14 trading days, using Wilder’s smoothing method.     ABOUT THE DATA

    Clear facts before appearance

    01LIVE TRADE

    A trade message from Finnhub with a price, stock and valid time. Only these messages qualify for the live-trade badge.

    02LATEST PRICE QUOTE

    A price and time from Finnhub’s quote service. It may stay the same when markets are closed. A quote is not a confirmed live trade.

    03HISTORICAL

    Past US daily prices from Massive, adjusted for stock splits, when set up. Otherwise, available Finnhub history or clearly labelled prices recorded on this Mac. Missing data stays missing.

    04MODEL ESTIMATE

    An estimated price range based on past daily price changes. It needs enough data and shows uncertainty. It is not a guarantee.
